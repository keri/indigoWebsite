[Artist Name] is a painter and sculptor working from a studio in [City, State]. Their practice is grounded in the physical encounter between material and mark — built-up surfaces of oil and wax are abraded, sanded, and repainted until the image arrives less through intention than excavation.

Recurring concerns include the geology of memory, the phenomenology of landscape, and the ethics of erasure. Works range from intimate panels to large-scale canvases, and occasionally extend into three dimensions through cast and assembled sculpture.

Before returning to studio practice full-time, [Artist Name] studied at [Institution] and spent several years working in [field/region], an experience that continues to inform both subject matter and working method.

Artist Statement

"I am interested in what a surface can hold — not as representation but as record. The paintings begin in accumulation and end in loss: layers applied and then destroyed, the image appearing in the gap between what was put down and what was taken away. I am not trying to picture the landscape so much as to use its logic — erosion, deposition, the slow weight of time on matter."
